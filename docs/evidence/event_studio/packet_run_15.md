## 7. Lessons from past events

- (what worked) Retain meaningful opportunities to engage with the mission: the dinner speaker prompted personal conversations, while resource tables and peer-led discussion drew positive feedback. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample), Campus Wellness Panel (sample)]
- (what worked) Reuse the dinner’s volunteer check-in approach and the walk’s team structure, while improving meal provision. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recurring problem) Food quotes, minimum guarantees, quantities and volunteer meals require separate attention; repeated problems involved both unexpected charges and people going without food. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample), Campus Wellness Panel (sample)]
- (recurring problem) Late sponsor materials, auction delivery and seating changes created avoidable pressure. Set explicit deadlines and owners for these dependencies. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) Apply the already-adopted lesson: agree whether the gala primarily serves fundraising, awareness or both before evaluating format and spending. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample), Campus Wellness Panel (sample)]
- (recommendation) Carry forward the reviewed proposal for a program under an hour that retains the recovery speaker. This responds to reported length concerns without discarding a praised element. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) Plan payment collection at the event rather than relying on follow-up, and assign responsibility for outstanding pledges. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) Check entrance accessibility and plan a welcoming experience for first-time guests. Do not assume that an expensive meal makes everyone feel included. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- **History check, passed:** Food-cost lesson respected (no binding catering minimum). No binding catering minimum at conservative attendance.
- (community: Welcome and inclusion) A board member observed inclusive participation at the walk; this is not a verified measure of everyone’s experience. “Families with strollers and older walkers joined; the one-mile loop meant nobody was left behind.” [Board observation (not a verified outcome); reflections 1]

- (community: Volunteer appreciation) Public recognition occurred, but inadequate food undermined practical volunteer care. “We thanked volunteers by name at the finish line, but their food ran short.” [Board observation (not a verified outcome); reflections 1]
