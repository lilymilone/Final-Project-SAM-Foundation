**Decisions for the board**

- Define whether the gala primarily serves fundraising, awareness, or both, and whether the $40,000 target is gross or net.
- Confirm whether the $18,000 cost cap and fundraising target are firm or should be explicitly reconsidered.
- Choose whether to prioritize revising Bueno’s seated dinner or reopen the venue and format search.
- Set spending priorities between the dinner experience and other elements; decide whether supporter and sponsored-access ticket tiers should be developed.

**Risks**

- Availability within the requested date window and wheelchair accessibility are unknown; both are prerequisites to selection.
- Catering minimums, guest-count changes, volunteer meals, and other contract terms could change the cost.
- Ticket sales, sponsorship, and donations are projections, not secured funds, and are unavailable for the modeled upfront payments.
- Recorded operating expenses exceed income. Protecting the reserve floor remains important even though the event model projects a surplus.
- Cutting the meal could affect the intended guest experience, but there is no evidence that a plated dinner produces higher giving.

**Next steps**

- Have a board-designated person review and personalize the existing unsent Bueno inquiry, then send it if appropriate. Request availability across the date range, written accessibility details, layout, speaker facilities, sound arrangements, and complete catering terms.
- Replace entertainment, marketing, staffing, and insurance allowances with quotes. Confirm payment timing, the reserve balance, and written sponsorship commitments before Finance reruns the affordability checks.
- Draft a program under an hour with a personal tribute to Sabrina, a short recovery talk, and a brief optional paddle raise. Keep the speaker’s recovery story separate from any expectation to solicit gifts.
- Use dedicated volunteer check-in roles; establish a guest-count lock two weeks out and seating, printing, and delivery deadlines. Test registration and sound before the event.
- If an auction is included, prepare table-payment collection rather than relying on later pledge follow-up. Seek in-kind support without counting prospective contributions as savings.
- Return the revised plan to the board with verified access, available dates, written costs, and Finance’s updated verdict.

## 7. Lessons from past events

- (what worked) Retain a personal recovery speaker as a candidate centerpiece: the dinner reflection calls the speaker the heart of the night. Resource-fair and panel feedback also supports programming that creates meaningful engagement. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample), Campus Wellness Panel (sample)]
- (what worked) Reuse the volunteer team structure and dedicated check-in approach praised in the walk and dinner reflections. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recurring problem) Food spending repeatedly exceeded expectations or involved overlooked terms; food availability also failed at the walk. Budget control and adequate service both need attention. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample), Campus Wellness Panel (sample)]
- (recurring problem) Late inputs and insufficient checks led to rush printing, rush delivery, registration problems, and seating changes. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) For the Manhattan gala, compare written catering terms and minimums before selecting a venue, obtain written per-head pricing, and establish a guest-count lock two weeks out. Do not assume cost control requires eliminating the plated dinner; that choice remains contested. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) Plan to collect auction payments at the table rather than relying on post-event follow-up, responding to the dinner's uncollected pledges. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) Apply the board's already-adopted lesson: agree whether the gala primarily serves fundraising, awareness, or both, and use that purpose to evaluate format and spending. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample), Campus Wellness Panel (sample)]
- (recommendation) Carry forward the already-reviewed proposal for a program under an hour while retaining the recovery speaker. This addresses reported length concerns without removing a praised element. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
