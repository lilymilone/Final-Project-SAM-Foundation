## 7. Lessons from past events

- (what worked) Preserve the personal recovery speaker: both the board reflection and reported guest feedback identify this as a central strength of the dinner. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (what worked) Reuse the volunteer team structure and dedicated check-in approach, which were praised across the walk and dinner. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recurring problem) Food budgets were vulnerable to volunteer meals, vendor minimum guarantees, guest-count changes, and venue food-and-beverage minimums. A per-head quote alone did not describe the full commitment. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recurring problem) Late sponsor artwork, auction-item delivery, and seating changes created rush work or complications. Set explicit deadlines and owners for these dependencies. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) Plan to collect auction payments at the table rather than relying on post-event follow-up; the dinner reflection reports that several pledges were never collected. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) Apply the already-adopted lesson: agree whether the gala primarily serves fundraising, awareness, or both, and evaluate format and spending against that purpose. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample), Campus Wellness Panel (sample)]
- (recommendation) Use the previously reviewed proposal for a program under an hour while retaining the recovery speaker. This addresses reported length concerns without treating that proposal as already adopted. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- **History check, passed:** Food-cost lesson respected (no binding catering minimum). No binding catering minimum at conservative attendance.
