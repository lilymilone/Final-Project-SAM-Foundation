## 7. Lessons from past events

- (what worked) Retain space for personal recovery stories and direct conversations. The dinner speaker and walk resource fair received positive reported feedback, and the campus partner requested another panel. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample), Campus Wellness Panel (sample)]
- (what worked) Preserve the volunteer check-in team and clear team structure; board members reported smooth dinner check-in and energized walk volunteers. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recurring problem) Food costs and availability repeatedly departed from expectations. Written pricing alone should not substitute for checking minimum guarantees, guest-count terms, and adequate meal quantities. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample), Campus Wellness Panel (sample)]
- (recurring problem) Volunteer recognition was not consistently matched by adequate food: walk volunteers ran short, and some dinner volunteers left hungry. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) For the proposed gala, compare written catering terms and minimums before venue selection, obtain the per-head price in writing, and plan to lock the guest count two weeks out. Keep the meal-format decision open for board discussion rather than treating cost reduction as agreed. [evidence: Spring Awareness Walk (sample), Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) Plan to collect auction payments at the table rather than relying on later follow-up. Strong pledges should not be treated as proof of collected revenue. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) Apply the already-adopted lesson: agree whether the gala primarily serves fundraising, awareness, or both before judging format and spending. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample), Campus Wellness Panel (sample)]
- (recommendation) Use the already-reviewed proposal to draft a program under an hour while retaining the recovery speaker. This responds to reported length concerns without discarding a praised element. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample)]
- (recommendation) Check step-free entry, consider how pricing may affect first-time guests, and provide a quieter place for conversation. These address reported barriers rather than assuming all guests experienced the dinner alike. [evidence: Butterfly Benefit Dinner (sample), Campus Wellness Panel (sample)]
- **History check, passed:** Food-cost lesson respected (no binding catering minimum). No binding catering minimum at conservative attendance.
- (community: Welcome and inclusion) One board member observed that the shorter walk route supported participation by families and older walkers; this is not a verified account of every attendee's experience. “Families with strollers and older walkers joined; the one-mile loop meant nobody was left behind.” [Board observation (not a verified outcome); reflections 1]
